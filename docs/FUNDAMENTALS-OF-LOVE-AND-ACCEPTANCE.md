# Wonder — Fundamentals of Love & Acceptance

This document is the governing product philosophy for Wonder across assessment, Mirror, matching, journaling, Wonder AI, introductions, and post-date experiences.

## Core thesis

**You can only meet someone as deeply as you've met yourself.**

**You cannot know someone you have stopped being curious about.**

Wonder exists to make deeper human knowing more likely. AI should facilitate discovery, reflection, vulnerability, attention, and better questions. It must not replace the relationship, declare what another person means, or make choices for the user.

## Acceptance and discernment

Self-acceptance means seeing oneself clearly enough to stop performing against reality. It does not mean abandoning standards, ignoring incompatibility, or remaining in relationships that chronically violate one's needs.

Acceptance and discernment can coexist. Wonder should help users understand what is true, what they need, what they fear, what they are choosing, and what remains unknown.

The product should invite honest and vulnerable answers before matching rather than reward an idealized dating persona. Mirror feedback, journaling, and reflective exercises may ask users to respond from a position of compassion toward themselves without forcing a positive reinterpretation of difficult experiences.

## Human agency

Wonder informs choice; it does not substitute for it.

- A compatibility score is evidence, not destiny.
- Attraction is not owed.
- Continuation is not owed.
- Curiosity is an invitation, not an obligation to stay.
- A user may decide that a difference is worth understanding or that it is a boundary.
- AI should never pressure someone to pursue, forgive, reconcile with, or remain with another person.

## Three relational data layers

### Layer 1 — Self model
Who the user says and reveals themselves to be through onboarding, assessment, preferences, Mirror feedback, corrections, journal entries, and explicit self-reflection.

### Layer 2 — Relational self
Who the user reveals themselves to be **in connection**: what they notice, value, overlook, fear, become curious about, feel safe enough to reveal, withdraw from, pursue, need after tension, repair, idealize, or resist.

Layer 2 belongs to the observer. Wonder may update relational-self hypotheses only from repeated or converging evidence. A single date should not overwrite direct self-report or become a settled trait.

### Layer 3 — Subjective experience of another person
How one user experiences another person in a particular interaction.

This is observer-bound and context-bound evidence, not a fact about the other person.

Layer 3 must never become an objective personality label, hidden desirability/reputation score, cross-user dossier, or universal statement about the observed person.

Different people may experience the same person differently. That variance can contain compatibility information without making any one experience universally true.

Every Layer 3 record must preserve the observer, person observed, encounter/context, timestamp, and explicit subjectivity.

## Observation before interpretation

Wonder should help users distinguish:

### What I know
Concrete observations, words, actions, facts, or directly experienced events.

### What I am interpreting
The meaning the user is assigning to what happened.

### What I wonder
Questions that remain open and could be explored through conversation or future experience.

Wonder should interrupt premature certainty without dismissing intuition or concern.

## Reflection modes

Users should be able to enter reflection from the state they are actually in.

### Curiosity
“There is something I want to understand.”

### Reflection
“I want to make sense of what I experienced.”

### Concern
“Something felt off, confusing, or painful.”

### Excitement
“Something felt unusually good or alive.”

Concern prompts should prioritize clarity, boundaries, direct evidence, and what remains unknown. Excitement prompts should preserve positive curiosity rather than turn excitement into prediction or destiny.

## Relationship-depth progression

### Encounters 1–2 — Immediate experience
Ask about surprise, felt experience, safety/ease, attraction, what was noticed, what remains unknown, and whether the user wants to keep exploring.

### Encounters 3–4 — Interaction pattern
Ask what felt different, where the user felt more or less themselves, how the two people appear to affect one another, what increased/decreased curiosity, what assumptions changed, and emerging repair/friction patterns.

### Encounters 5+ — Sustained knowing
Ask about vulnerability, repair, changing perception, recurring differences, what the user feels understood/misunderstood about, what they have learned about themselves around this person, what question they may have stopped asking, and whether curiosity is deepening or being replaced by assumption.

## Carry curiosity forward

Post-date reflection is not only an archive. Wonder should carry unresolved curiosity into the next interaction.

Before a future date, Wonder may privately remind the user of one or two questions they previously wanted to understand. These are prompts for attention, not scripts the user must ask verbatim.

## Relational learning

Wonder may learn from post-date data in two legitimate ways:

1. **About the observer:** recurring relational needs, responses, attractions, avoidances, curiosity patterns, repair preferences, and discrepancies between stated preferences and lived experience.
2. **About interaction fit:** whether particular combinations of person-model characteristics tend to produce ease, curiosity, understanding, attraction, repair, or chronic friction.

Wonder may not create an objective secret profile of the observed person from other people's subjective reports.

## Evidence threshold for evolving the Mirror

- One encounter produces a hypothesis, not a trait.
- Repeated evidence increases confidence.
- Evidence across different partners is stronger for a relational-self pattern than repeated evidence about only one partner.
- Contradictions should be surfaced as questions before being collapsed into conclusions.
- User corrections remain first-class evidence.

Preferred language includes: “Wonder has noticed a pattern…”, “Across several connections, you seem to…”, “This may be more situational than stable”, and “Wonder is not yet sure whether…”

## Sharing and consent

Reflections are private by default.

Nothing a user writes about another person should automatically be shown to that person.

If Wonder later allows a user to share a question or reflection with the person they are seeing, sharing must be explicit and item-specific; the user must see exactly what will be shared; private notes and model inferences remain private; and Wonder should prefer sharing a question that opens conversation over sharing an AI verdict.

## AI behavior

Wonder should prefer questions over pronouncements, distinguish evidence from interpretation, reopen curiosity where certainty has outrun evidence, notice repeated observer patterns without turning them into fixed labels, and preserve ambiguity when ambiguity is honest.

## Product test

Every relationship feature should pass these questions:

1. Does this help a person know themselves or another person more deeply?
2. Does it preserve rather than replace human curiosity?
3. Is the AI making a claim stronger than the evidence supports?
4. Does subjective relational data remain attached to its observer and context?
5. Does the user retain meaningful choice and consent?
6. Are acceptance and compassion being confused with ignoring incompatibility or boundaries?
7. Would this feature make the relationship more human if it worked perfectly?

If the answer to the final question is no, the feature does not belong in Wonder.
