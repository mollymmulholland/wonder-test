# Wonder Archetype System v1

## Purpose
Wonder archetypes are relational pattern models, not personality labels, diagnoses, compatibility guarantees, or fixed identities. The assessment measures dimensions first; archetypes compress those dimensions into a legible higher-order pattern. Because archetypes are now the primary matching representation, they must be treated as testable product hypotheses and calibrated against observed relationship outcomes.

## The eight archetypes

### Architect
**Organizing motive:** make complexity coherent and build something durable.
**Relational gift:** reliability, intentionality, problem solving, follow-through.
**Relational need:** respect for competence plus enough emotional access that the relationship does not become purely functional.
**Growth edge:** control can substitute for vulnerability; optimization can crowd out discovery.
**Elemental signature:** Earth + Air.
**Prototype emphasis:** cognitive systemizing, structure, competence, achievement, stability, decisiveness, repair through action.

### Seer
**Organizing motive:** perceive what is beneath the obvious and make meaning from complexity.
**Relational gift:** nuance, psychological perception, curiosity, emotional/intellectual depth.
**Relational need:** to be taken seriously without being forced into premature certainty.
**Growth edge:** interpretation can become projection; insight can replace direct communication.
**Elemental signature:** Air + Ether + Water.
**Prototype emphasis:** contextual cognition, ambiguity tolerance, meaning, knowledge, vulnerability, emotional depth.

### Explorer
**Organizing motive:** encounter what is not yet known through experience.
**Relational gift:** openness, novelty, play, expansion, adaptive curiosity.
**Relational need:** room to move without the bond being treated as disposable.
**Growth edge:** novelty can become avoidance of depth, routine, or responsibility.
**Elemental signature:** Air + Fire.
**Prototype emphasis:** novelty, freedom, ambiguity tolerance, social initiative, autonomy, curiosity.

### Sovereign
**Organizing motive:** exercise agency and shape consequential outcomes.
**Relational gift:** direction, protection, decisiveness, ambition, capacity to act.
**Relational need:** mutual respect and a partner with a self strong enough not to disappear into them.
**Growth edge:** agency can become control; admiration can be mistaken for intimacy.
**Elemental signature:** Fire + Earth.
**Prototype emphasis:** decisiveness, influence, achievement, competence, autonomy, directness, stress control.

### Alchemist
**Organizing motive:** transform experience into something more alive, meaningful, or beautiful.
**Relational gift:** intensity, imagination, reinvention, erotic/creative vitality, emotional range.
**Relational need:** a relationship capable of holding change without confusing intensity for instability.
**Growth edge:** transformation can become volatility; intensity can be mistaken for compatibility.
**Elemental signature:** Fire + Water + Ether.
**Prototype emphasis:** emotional intensity, novelty, meaning, beauty, vulnerability, distinctiveness, contextual cognition.

### Devotee
**Organizing motive:** love through sustained investment, loyalty, and emotional presence.
**Relational gift:** intimacy, tenderness, commitment, reciprocity, active repair.
**Relational need:** evidence that care is mutual rather than assumed.
**Growth edge:** devotion can become over-accommodation, fusion, or vigilance toward imbalance.
**Elemental signature:** Water + Earth.
**Prototype emphasis:** closeness, loyalty, family, reciprocity, vulnerability, repair, belonging.

### Guardian
**Organizing motive:** create safety, continuity, and a dependable place for life to grow.
**Relational gift:** steadiness, trustworthiness, containment, practical care.
**Relational need:** security that still leaves room for evolution and individuality.
**Growth edge:** protection can become rigidity; stability can become resistance to necessary change.
**Elemental signature:** Earth + Water.
**Prototype emphasis:** stability, family, loyalty, trust, structure, repair, moderate closeness.

### Maverick
**Organizing motive:** preserve authorship of the self and resist living by inherited scripts.
**Relational gift:** independence, originality, courage, unconventional perspective.
**Relational need:** intimacy without possession and acceptance without domestication.
**Growth edge:** autonomy can become defensive distance; distinctiveness can become reflexive opposition.
**Elemental signature:** Fire + Air.
**Prototype emphasis:** autonomy, freedom, distinctiveness, novelty, ambiguity tolerance, lower structure dependence.

## Primary + secondary archetype
The primary archetype describes the strongest organizing pattern currently supported by the assessment. The secondary archetype modifies how that pattern is expressed. Wonder should display the blend as a dynamic pattern, not a compound identity label.

Examples:
- Architect + Seer: structure in service of understanding.
- Architect + Devotee: structure in service of commitment and care.
- Sovereign + Maverick: agency strongly organized around autonomy.
- Alchemist + Devotee: intensity organized around attachment and transformation.
- Guardian + Explorer: security with an active need for expansion.

The primary/secondary score gap determines blend strength. Close scores should be treated as a genuine hybrid rather than forcing a dominant identity.

## Pairing model
There are no universal 'best archetype couples.' Wonder evaluates a pairing across five archetypal relationship functions:

1. **Recognition** — does each archetype naturally recognize something central in the other?
2. **Support** — can each provide conditions the other tends to need?
3. **Expansion** — does difference create useful growth rather than simple mismatch?
4. **Repairability** — when the archetypal shadows collide, is repair structurally plausible?
5. **Life coherence** — can the pair plausibly build compatible lives rather than only generate chemistry?

The current matching engine approximates these through prototype dimensions. These are hypotheses to calibrate, not scientific claims.

## Pair hypotheses worth testing
These are directional hypotheses for research and QA, not hard-coded destiny rules.

**Architect × Seer:** potentially high recognition and cognitive expansion. Risk: one demands closure while the other preserves ambiguity.

**Architect × Devotee:** potentially strong durability. Risk: practical problem solving may fail to satisfy emotional bids.

**Architect × Explorer:** potentially productive structure/novelty complementarity. Risk: planning and spontaneity become mutual criticism.

**Sovereign × Seer:** potentially strong agency/insight complementarity. Risk: certainty versus nuance, or power imbalance.

**Sovereign × Devotee:** potentially strong commitment and direction when reciprocity is high. Risk: devotion becomes accommodation and agency becomes dominance.

**Sovereign × Maverick:** potentially high respect, independence, and vitality. Risk: autonomy contests and difficulty yielding.

**Alchemist × Guardian:** potentially valuable transformation/stability complementarity. Risk: Guardian experiences change as chaos; Alchemist experiences stability as confinement.

**Alchemist × Devotee:** potentially unusually deep emotional recognition. Risk: intensity loops, fusion, or treating emotional magnitude as proof of fit.

**Explorer × Guardian:** potentially grounding and expansion in both directions. Risk: security versus novelty becomes a recurring negotiation.

**Explorer × Maverick:** potentially high freedom and discovery. Risk: insufficient containment, follow-through, or mutual dependence.

**Devotee × Maverick:** potentially powerful closeness/autonomy complementarity when secure. Risk: pursuit-distance dynamics if devotion is read as possession or autonomy as rejection.

**Guardian × Guardian:** potentially high safety and life coherence. Risk: excessive sameness, avoidance of necessary disruption, or low novelty.

**Seer × Seer:** potentially exceptional recognition and meaning-making. Risk: mutual interpretation without enough concrete action.

**Sovereign × Sovereign:** potentially high respect and shared ambition. Risk: control contests unless both have strong repair and mutuality.

Same-archetype pairs are not inherently inferior. Complementarity is useful only when the difference is supportable.

## Shadow collision rules
Archetype compatibility should be downgraded when measured behavior suggests that the likely shadow interaction is active. Examples:

- Devotee reassurance need + Maverick/Explorer withdrawal -> pursuit-distance risk.
- Sovereign control + Maverick autonomy -> agency conflict.
- Alchemist intensity + low repair -> volatility risk.
- Architect control + Seer ambiguity -> closure/interpretation conflict.
- Guardian stability + Explorer novelty -> constriction/restlessness conflict.
- Two high-intensity archetypes + low trust/repair -> chemistry should not be mistaken for compatibility.

These modifiers should use measured dimensions rather than archetype names alone.

## Match rationale language
Wonder should never tell users that two archetypes 'belong together.' The product should explain the relationship hypothesis.

Preferred structure:
- **Why Wonder noticed you:** the central archetypal recognition.
- **What may feel unusually easy:** one or two supported relationship functions.
- **What this pairing asks of you:** the most relevant shadow collision or growth requirement.
- **Why we still chose the introduction:** measured evidence that the tension is supportable.

## Calibration loop
For every introduction, store the primary/secondary archetypes and archetype scores for both users at match time. After interaction, collect outcome evidence including mutual interest, meeting completion, second-date desire, felt understood, attraction, emotional safety, conversational ease, intellectual stimulation, values fit, and rejection reason.

Do not optimize only for first-date attraction. The eventual target should distinguish at least:
- initial resonance,
- sustained curiosity,
- relational safety,
- mutual investment,
- second/third-date continuation,
- longer-term relationship formation.

Pair hypotheses should change only when enough outcome data exists. Early qualitative feedback can inform product research but should not be mistaken for calibrated evidence.

## Product principle
Archetypes make the person legible. They do not replace the person.

The matching system should use archetypes to answer **why these two people may matter to each other**, and measured relational evidence to answer **whether that possibility is safe and viable enough to introduce**.
