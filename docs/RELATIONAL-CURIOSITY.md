# Wonder Relational Curiosity System

## Foundational principles

**You can only meet someone as deeply as you've met yourself.**

**You cannot know someone you have stopped being curious about.**

Wonder should facilitate discovery, not replace it.

## Three data layers

### Layer 1: self model
What a user says and reveals about themselves through onboarding, the psychometric instrument, Mirror feedback, journal entries, preferences, and explicit corrections.

### Layer 2: relational self
What the user reveals about themselves in connection: what they notice, value, overlook, fear, feel safe with, withdraw from, become curious about, repair, idealize, or resist.

Post-date reflections may update hypotheses about the **observer's** relational model when evidence accumulates, but should not overwrite direct self-report from a single encounter.

### Layer 3: subjective experience of another person
How one person experiences another person in a specific interaction.

This layer is intentionally subjective. It must never become:
- an objective personality label assigned to the other person;
- a hidden attractiveness, safety, quality, or reputation score;
- a cross-user dossier;
- a claim that "people experience this person as X."

The same individual may evoke ease in one person and uncertainty in another. That variance can be useful compatibility information without making either observation universally true.

## Post-date progression

### Early encounters (1–2)
Focus on immediate experience and curiosity:
- What surprised you?
- How did you feel in their presence?
- What did you notice?
- What do you want to know that you still do not know?
- Do you want to keep exploring?

### Developing connection (3–4)
Focus on emerging interaction patterns:
- What are you beginning to understand about how you affect each other?
- What felt different this time?
- Where did you feel more or less yourself?
- What are you more curious about now than before?

### Established connection (5+)
Focus on vulnerability, repair, changing perception, and sustained curiosity:
- Where did you feel especially understood or misunderstood?
- What tension or difference feels worth understanding rather than solving?
- What has changed in how you see them?
- What has changed in how you experience yourself around them?
- What question about them have you stopped asking that may be worth reopening?

## Product behavior

Wonder should prefer questions over pronouncements. Good prompts reopen attention rather than tell the user what the relationship means.

Bad:
> They are emotionally unavailable.

Better:
> You have described feeling less certain when they become quieter. What do you actually know about what their quiet means, and what are you filling in yourself?

Bad:
> This is an incompatible match.

Better:
> You seem to want more explicit repair after tension than you have experienced so far. Is that a difference the two of you can understand and negotiate, or does it leave you chronically unmet?

## Learning loop

Relational observations can improve matching in two ways:
1. learning more about the observer's relational needs and patterns;
2. learning interaction-specific compatibility patterns between two people or between classes of person-model configurations.

They must not be used to create an objective reputation model of the observed person.

## Epistemic rule

Every stored relational observation should preserve:
- who observed it;
- who they were interacting with;
- the encounter context;
- that it is subjective;
- when it occurred.

Never strip the observation from its observer and context and promote it into a universal statement about the other person.

## Long-term product goal

The assessment helps Wonder understand the user before connection. The post-date system helps the user continue understanding themselves and another person through connection.

The product should become more useful after a match, not disappear once two people meet.
